Why do I people please? The answer is in your body

By Jennifer Jones 8 minute read Updated July 2026

You are not nice. You are scared. And there is a difference your body has been keeping track of for years.

People pleasing gets talked about like a personality quirk. Something you laugh about. "Oh, I just hate confrontation." But the women who come to me carrying this pattern are not describing a preference. They are describing a survival strategy they built when they were small, in a house where somebody else's mood decided whether the day was safe.

That strategy worked. That is the part nobody says out loud. It kept you out of the line of fire. The problem is you are thirty five now, and it is still running, and it is costing you things you have not connected to it yet.

What people pleasing actually is

Kindness is a choice you make from a place of having options. People pleasing is a threat response. You are not choosing to say yes. You are managing a danger your nervous system believes is still in the room.

Researchers have a name for the specific version of this that shows up in relationships. They call it self silencing, meaning you suppress what you actually think or feel in order to keep the connection intact.

Here is how you know the difference

  • You say yes and feel a drop in your stomach immediately after
  • You rehearse conversations before you have them, sometimes for days
  • You apologize for things that are not yours to apologize for
  • Someone else being upset feels like an emergency you have to fix right now
  • You cannot answer the question "what do you want" without first checking what they want
  • You feel relief, not joy, when a plan gets cancelled

If you read that list and felt caught, keep going. The next part is the part that usually stops people.

What it is doing to your body

This is not a metaphor. There is measurable, published research on what happens to a body that swallows itself for years.

4x

Women who silenced themselves during conflict with their spouse were four times more likely to die over a ten year follow up than women who spoke up. This held even after researchers accounted for age, blood pressure, cholesterol, and smoking.

Framingham Offspring Study, 3,682 adults followed for ten years

Four times. Not four percent. And it was not the cigarettes, because they controlled for that. Self silencing was also linked to higher rates of carotid plaque, the arterial buildup that leads to stroke and heart attack.

35%

People in the highest range for emotional suppression had a 35 percent higher risk of dying from any cause and a 70 percent higher risk of dying from cancer over a twelve year follow up.

General Social Survey linked to the National Death Index

Here is the mechanism, in plain language. Every time you override your own no, your body runs the full stress response. Heart rate up, cortisol released, muscles braced. But then nothing happens. There is no fight. There is no leaving. There is a smile and a yes and a change of subject.

So the stress response never completes. It just sits there. Do that a few thousand times over fifteen years and you flatten your natural cortisol rhythm, which research links directly to higher inflammation markers in the blood. Inflammation is the road to heart disease, autoimmune conditions, and the exhaustion you cannot sleep off.

You are not tired because you did too much. You are tired because you did too much for people who were never going to ask if you were okay.

Why boundaries do not fix it

You have read the boundary scripts. You may have even memorized a few. And then the moment comes and your mouth says yes anyway, and afterward you are furious at yourself, which is its own second injury.

Here is why the scripts do not hold. A boundary is a fence around something you believe is worth protecting. If underneath everything you do not actually believe you are worth protecting, you are building a fence around an empty lot. The fence has no job. So it falls down.

That is why this is identity work and not behavior work. You cannot script your way out of a belief about your own worth. You have to go get the belief.

Where the belief came from

Nobody is born a people pleaser. Somewhere there was a moment, or more likely a thousand small moments, where you learned that your needs made the room harder. Maybe you had a parent whose mood was weather. Maybe you were the responsible one. Maybe you were praised for being easy, and you understood early that easy was the price of being loved.

None of that was your fault. All of it became your operating system.

The excavation is not about blaming anybody. It is about going back to the moment the belief was installed, seeing it clearly as a child's reasonable conclusion, and then noticing that you are not in that house anymore.

Three things you can do this week

1. Notice the drop

You do not have to change your answer yet. Just notice the physical sensation that comes right after you say yes to something you did not want. That drop is information. Your body has been telling you the truth this whole time. Start listening to it before you try to obey it.

2. Buy yourself twenty four hours

The sentence is "let me check and get back to you." That is it. You are not saying no. You are interrupting the automatic yes long enough for your actual preference to surface. Most people pleasers have never met their own preference because it never got a chance to speak.

3. Track one week of yeses

Write down every yes you give for seven days. Next to each one, write who it served. At the end of the week, count how many served you. That number is usually the thing that makes women stop negotiating with themselves.

The honest part

You are going to read this, recognize yourself, feel something crack open, and then most likely go right back to the pattern by Thursday. Not because you are weak. Because awareness is not the same thing as interruption, and a pattern this old does not dissolve because you finally named it.

Naming it is step one of six. It matters. But by itself it changes nothing, and I would rather tell you that than sell you a feeling.

What actually moves this is doing the excavation with structure, with somebody who can see the pattern when you cannot, and alongside other women who are dismantling the same thing. That is what the twelve weeks are for.
